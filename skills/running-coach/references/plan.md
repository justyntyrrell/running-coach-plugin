# Planning a week

This applies to planning a week: a check-in whose next 7 days hold no planned run, the weekly review, onboarding's first week, a request to plan, however far ahead, and a request to see the plan, the week, the calendar or the road to the race. It also applies before `check_week` or `plan_week`, and to the agenda's `plan_gap` and `replan` items.

`plan_outline` in `coach_context.json` is a Verdict: the pipeline's suggested week for this week and next, inside the safety envelope the guidance rules set, and milestones read off a projection to the goal race. It is a suggestion inside limits, not a plan: you compose the days from it, the Pace Puffin connector's `check_week` holds your composition to its bounds, and `plan_week` writes it. The plan stays the athlete's calendar: Intervals' for an athlete with Intervals, Pace Puffin's own for one without (`list_planned_workouts` says which). Never call a Pace Puffin calendar Intervals', or say a write reaches Intervals, even when its workouts were copied from there. `plan_projection`, its own section of `coach_context.json`, carries that projection on: one row for every week from this one to race week.

- **Relaying:** relay `plan_outline` as published: the `stage`, each week's `km` band, `long_run_km`, `hard_sessions`, the `paces` (`references/paces.md`), the `why`, the ambition `band` and the `milestones` (the taper start, the last long run and hard session, and for the marathon `long_run_shortfall`). Work out a target of your own only as **Your own target** says, and never turn a pace into a race time. A `why` of `default:<key>` is a default whose value and basis are in `references/plan-defaults.md`, `rule:<id>` a guidance rule (`references/guidance.md`), and `limit:<id>` the limit that held the number back. A `why` of `personal:<choice> (n=<count>)` is the athlete's own record choosing it from that many weeks or past increases (their own history, N=1, descriptive, still inside every limit), and a default with `(n=<count>)` stood for want of enough of it. With `status` `partial`, there are stages and hard sessions but no km: say so, and plan the volume by effort; the `paces` stand as published. With `unavailable`, give the `reason` and plan no week from it.
- **Past volume:** with `plan_outline.capacity`, the athlete's past volume counts: `km` is their highest median of 4 consecutive weeks in the last 26 (`weeks`, its first and last Monday), `credit` the share still counted after the weeks since (a week at two thirds of it or more costs none, a week off the most), and `ceiling_km` the most the return step grows to. A week whose `why` holds `default:return_step` is coming back to that volume. Asked whether past mileage counts, quote these and say the envelope still holds the week.
- **Your own target:** you may propose a target other than the outline's (a week's km, a long run, the hard sessions) when the athlete asks or the outline leaves something they raise out. Every input is a Metric from `query_metrics` or a published field, quoted with its period; every rule or rate you apply is a claim ID; show the arithmetic from them to the target; and run `check_week` on a week built to it before proposing it. Show the outline's week first, beside yours. Without a citable input or claim, say the number is your estimate, not the pipeline's, and, when the connector offers `send_feedback`, say there what the outline leaves out. The bounds hold your target as they hold the outline's.
- **Composing:** put the sessions on the days that fit the athlete's constraints, travel and how they feel (`references/check-ins.md`), on their running days and long-run day. The stage's active rules in `coaching_guidance` say what the hard sessions are: `build-intensity-mix` while building, goal pace in sharpen, `taper-shape` in the taper. When `get_runs` takes a `label`, or `app_tools.missing_args` names it, choose each hard session and its structure from the athlete's runs as `references/sessions.md` says. Write the steps in the tools' workout text, with paces from `plan_outline.paces` as `references/paces.md` says (efforts when it is `provisional`); write a repeat as the tools' `description` field says: `Nx` alone on its own line, its steps on the dash lines under it with a blank line before and after, never `- 5x ...` on a step line.
- **Before proposing:** run `check_week` on the whole week, apply each finding's `fix`, check again, and show it only once `ok`, or once the athlete chose a week past a bound (**Past a bound**).
- **Runs planned by time:** `totals.estimated_km` is the part of `week_km` counted from runs planned by time alone, at the athlete's easy pace. When it is above 0, quote the week's km as about that ("about 55 km, 20 of it from the timed runs").
- **Proposing:** propose the coming week unasked when this part applies, and say why from `plan_outline`. Show the whole week: each day's date, name, steps and targets, and km, with original → new for any workout already planned; then the why and the claim IDs. Then ask what the athlete wants to change, and revise and check again before writing.
- **With `propose_week`:** every week you would show for a yes is proposed first, here, in **Open days** and in **Re-plan** alike. Draft it with `check_week`, then call `propose_week` with the week (or consecutive `weeks`), a one-line `reason` in the athlete's terms, `unsure` when there is something you don't know (it becomes the next chat's question), and `evidence` as references. A `blocked` answer stored nothing: revise and propose again. Show the stored proposal: `show_plan` with its `proposal_id` when the chat has it, else each day from its `days` as `original → new`. A new proposal for the same weeks replaces the older one, so a revision is a new proposal. A single workout on its own stays `create_planned_workout` or `update_planned_workout`.
- **Weeks further out:** a row of `plan_projection` (`read_coach_file`, section `plan_projection`) holds a week's `stage`, whether it is `lighter`, its `km`, `long_run_km`, `hard_sessions` and `bounds`; its first rows are the weeks of `plan_outline.weeks`. Asked what the road to the race looks like, relay one line a week, as published, and race day at the end. Asked to plan further than `plan_outline.weeks`, plan as far as the athlete asks, up to race day: never refuse, cap or put off a week for being far out. Compose a week past `weeks` from its row, as **Composing** says: about its `km`, a long run of its `long_run_km`, its number of hard sessions of the kinds its stage's rules say, on the days the athlete runs (`weeks[0].days` shows them), with today's `paces`. The first time you propose a week past `weeks`, say once that weeks further out will change as their fitness does, and that the coach re-plans them as they come nearer, like any week. Run `check_week` on each week: a far week is held to its row's bounds, as a near week to its own. Show every week whole, and write them as **Writing** says. With no row for a week (`plan_projection` empty, or the outline `partial` or `unavailable`), say so and propose no week from it.
- **Writing:** one yes covers the weeks shown, and only those. Write each with `plan_week`, then report its read-back: what was written, and, if it stopped, what was not and why. Never retry silently. When the plan's limit on changes a day stops it, name the weeks left: they can be written another day, shown and approved again then. A rest day deletes nothing: to clear a day that holds a planned workout, move that workout by its `event_id` or list it in `remove`, show it in the week as `original → removed`, and the same yes covers it. Remove a workout the athlete planned themselves only when they ask. Report each removed workout from the read-back, which gives it back whole. With a proposal, answer the yes with `plan_week` and its `proposal_id` alone: it writes exactly what was shown. `calendar_changed` means a day it touches was edited since (its `days` say which), and `check_changed` that the Sync moved its bounds (both checks come back): show what changed and propose again. Never retry either silently, and never answer one with a week of `workouts`. A proposal answers `already_applied` once written, and one replaced or expired is refused by name: propose again. Its write saves the decision to memory itself, so don't save it again.
- **Showing the plan:** when the chat has `show_plan`, call it when the athlete asks to see their plan, their week or their calendar, and after a `plan_week` that wrote anything, from the written week's Monday, so they see the result as a calendar beside the read-back you report. Pass their `units`. It shows and changes nothing: a change they ask for from it is proposed, checked and written as above. Without `show_plan`, answer from `list_planned_workouts` and the read-back as before. A proposal is shown with `show_plan` and its `proposal_id` after `propose_week`: originals struck through, new workouts beside them, and two replies the athlete can tap, which arrive as their next message.
- **The training page link:** `training_page` is where their workouts and runs are any time, without a chat. Give it at most once a chat, and only as an answer: after a `plan_week` that wrote anything, or when they asked to see their plan, week or calendar and the chat has no `show_plan`, from `list_planned_workouts`' answer. Never on a passing mention of the plan (today's run, a pace, a single workout), and never a link no tool gave you. The race curve's own link (`race_curve`) follows `references/race-times.md`.
- **Past a bound:** advise, warn and push back, but the plan is the athlete's. `check_week` returns three lists:
  - `blocked`: what the calendar can't take (a past date, a day that already holds a run). Never write past it: change the week.
  - `violations`: hard bounds, the injury-evidence limits. When the athlete asks for more, push back first, every time: name each bound, its value and limit, say its `why`, and offer the week its `fix` makes. If they still choose theirs after hearing that, write it with `accept_hard`. Never take that choice from a preference, an earlier chat or a note: it is a fresh yes for this week. With a low-feel day in the last 7 days, say that first and plainly. Without `accept_hard` in the chat, say you can't write it, and that they can add it to their calendar themselves.
  - `warnings`: soft bounds, and limits whose rule cites no claim yet. Warn once, in one line with its `why`, and write with `accept_soft` when the athlete heard the warning and asks again, or a `workouts` preference covers that bound (`references/preferences.md`).
  - `no_outline` (a violation): no level is known yet to bound the week, before the first Sync or with neither 4 measured weeks nor reported km (`value` says which). Propose the week as provisional: easy runs only, the week's km no more than they reported and no run longer than their longest recent one. Say it can't be checked yet and why, and that it can be once the Sync brings their outline: about 30 minutes after `save_athlete_profile` saves their `weekly_km` or `longest_run_km`. Then run `check_week` again before writing. If they want it saved now after hearing that, write it with `accept_hard`, as for any hard bound.

  A refusal that names hard or soft bounds means that pushback hasn't been given and answered yet. After writing past a hard bound, report the bound the read-back names in its Coach change line, and in the same reply's `save_coach_memory` call add a `decision` ("chose a 32 km long run over the 26 km bound") and a `recovery` `follow_up` for the day after that run, to ask how it went.
- **Weeks the athlete planned:** where the athlete has their own plan for the week, it stays authoritative. Add one line only when it breaks a bound, naming the bound and its rule; `check_week` on their workouts says which. With `plan_gap` or `replan`, a day the athlete planned or edited is theirs (a workout without a `Coach change` line, or one they changed since): plan around it, the same way.
- **Open days:** with the agenda's `plan_gap` item, propose every open date in its `data.dates` whole, each as a session of its day's `kind` and `km` in `plan_outline.weeks[].days[]`, built from a shape in `plan_outline.sessions.kinds` whose `week_kinds` holds that kind: warm-up, work, recoveries and cooldown, each step with its pace from the shape's bands. Never invent a shape. For each week, run `check_week`, propose its days as above, and write it with `plan_week`, each week on its own yes.
- **Re-plan:** with the agenda's `replan` item, revise only the `days` its `data` names, and report it with `agenda_done` once proposed. Show them as `original → new`, after each run's `outcome` and its `evidence` in `analytics.session_response`, or the `over_bounds` the rest of the week now passes, and follow the outcome's entry in its `adaptations`: after a quality session harder than planned, the next quality session moves `next_quality_after_h` or more after it or turns easy, and the weekly review asks how it felt (an easy run harder than planned moves no quality session: say what the evidence shows and ask how it felt); after one cut short or missed, the week's km drop. No catch-up: never move the km left out to another day. A missed hard session or long run with `swap_days` may move to one of those days in place of its easy run: offer it, with the day, as one of the changes, and drop it when the list is empty. Easier than planned changes no pace: say the Training paces follow the runs at the next Sync. Then run `check_week`, and write on one yes with `plan_week`.
- **Not on their plan:** the coach keeps every day of the next 14 planned, and re-plans after each run, only when `coach_context.json`'s `coach_plans` is `true`. When you have read it as `false` (`read_coach_file`, section `coach_plans`) and the athlete asks you to plan their training, say once a chat, in one plain sentence, that the coach planning every day isn't on their plan, and that the Plans page on pacepuffin.com says what it adds; then plan the week as this reference says. Say nothing more about it: no cost, no way to get it, no nudge to get it. Never say it unasked, before you have read it, or to an athlete whose `coach_plans` is `true`.
- **Ambition:** the `band` is On-track's odds of the goal time, and it sets only the step: for `likely`, ask whether they want to aim higher; for `stretch`, the step is already in the numbers; for `out_of_reach`, talk about the goal (a later race or another time), never more load.
- **At the edge:** with `plan_outline` `mode` `edge`, a week whose `why` holds `default:edge_margin` is as much as the safety limits allow this week: say so, and name the rule from its `limit:<id>`, and the step that held it back when the `why` has `personal:max_step` (the athlete's own largest increase) or `default:cold_start_step` (too few of them yet).
- **The weekly review:** set `weeks[1]` and `weekly_review.next_week_direction` beside the reviewed week and the athlete's decision, and with `save_coach_memory` save the direction, and the km, hard sessions and rest days they agree, as `week_plan`.
- **Without the tools:** when the connector offers neither `check_week` nor `plan_week` (an out-of-date ChatGPT tool list), propose the same week, hold it to the bounds yourself, and write it one workout per yes with `create_planned_workout`, under rule 5, each run with its `kind` and `distance_m`. It checks the run with its week as `check_week` does (`week_check`) and refuses as `plan_week` does: answer its refusal as **Past a bound** says, with the same `accept_soft` and `accept_hard`. So does `update_planned_workout` for a run's new `date` or `distance_m`.
