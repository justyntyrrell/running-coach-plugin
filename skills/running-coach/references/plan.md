# Planning a week

This applies to planning a week: a check-in whose next 7 days hold no planned run, the weekly review, onboarding's first week, a request to plan, and a question about what pace or heart rate to run. It also applies before `check_week` or `plan_week`, and to the agenda's `plan_gap` and `replan` items.

`plan_outline` in `coach_context.json` is a Verdict: the pipeline's suggested week for this week and next, inside the safety envelope the guidance rules set, and milestones read off a projection to the goal race. It is a suggestion inside limits, not a plan: you compose the days from it, the Running coach connector's `check_week` holds your composition to its bounds, and `plan_week` writes it. The plan stays the athlete's Intervals calendar.

- **Relaying:** relay `plan_outline` as published: the `stage`, each week's `km` band, `long_run_km`, `hard_sessions`, the `paces` (below), the `why`, the ambition `band` and the `milestones` (the taper start, the last long run and hard session, and for the marathon `long_run_shortfall`). Work out a target of your own only as **Your own target** says, and never turn a pace into a race time. A `why` of `default:<key>` is a default whose value and basis are in `references/plan-defaults.md`, `rule:<id>` a guidance rule (`references/guidance.md`), and `limit:<id>` the limit that held the number back. A `why` of `personal:<choice> (n=<count>)` is the athlete's own record choosing it from that many weeks or past increases (their own history, N=1, descriptive, still inside every limit), and a default with `(n=<count>)` stood for want of enough of it. With `status` `partial`, there are stages and hard sessions but no km: say so, and plan the volume by effort; the `paces` stand as published. With `unavailable`, give the `reason` and plan no week from it.
- **Past volume:** with `plan_outline.capacity`, the athlete's past volume counts: `km` is their highest median of 4 consecutive weeks in the last 26 (`weeks`, its first and last Monday), `credit` the share still counted after the weeks since (a week at two thirds of it or more costs none, a week off the most), and `ceiling_km` the most the return step grows to. A week whose `why` holds `default:return_step` is coming back to that volume. Asked whether past mileage counts, quote these and say the envelope still holds the week.
- **Your own target:** you may propose a target other than the outline's (a week's km, a long run, the hard sessions) when the athlete asks or the outline leaves something they raise out. Every input is a Metric from `query_metrics` or a published field, quoted with its period; every rule or rate you apply is a claim ID; show the arithmetic from them to the target; and run `check_week` on a week built to it before proposing it. Show the outline's week first, beside yours. Without a citable input or claim, say the number is your estimate, not the pipeline's, and, when the connector offers `send_feedback`, say there what the outline leaves out. The bounds hold your target as they hold the outline's.
- **Training paces:** `paces` are the Training paces, what to run in each band: `bands` in seconds per km, [fastest, slowest], and `heart_rate`, with T's top as the ceiling `hr_ceiling_bpm`. Relay them with their `basis` (the `sessions` or the `race`, each moved to `elevation.home_m`), with `return` when it is `active` (T and I are the slow half of their range; say `days_since_race` and `days_since_hard_session`), and with `lthr` when the flags hold `lthr_above_race_hr`. The Anchor (`analytics.training_log.current_anchor`, `anchors.current` in `training_log.json`) is what runs are labelled against, never a target, and neither is critical speed. Across a day in `anchors.jumps`, compare paces, not Labels: a Label from before it rests on another Anchor and is no evidence of intensity today.
- **A pace question:** asked what pace or heart rate to run, in planning or not, give the band's range from `plan_outline.paces.bands` and its `heart_rate`; say in one line what it rests on (sessions, race, elevation, `return`). For T, name the last session in `sessions.list`, with its `date`, rep pace (`pace_sec_per_km`) and `last_rep_hr`, and give `hr_ceiling_bpm` as the heart-rate ceiling. `sessions.list` holds threshold sessions only, whatever their `label`: for another band, name none from it, and its ceiling is the top of its own `heart_rate`. Never quote `current_anchor` or critical speed as a target. With `status` `provisional`, give the `efforts` instead.
- **LTHR:** with `lthr_above_race_hr`, say once a chat, when you quote heart rate, that the Intervals LTHR setting (`lthr.setting`) looks high against the race of `lthr.race_date`, which averaged `lthr.race_hr`, and that the heart rate here is built on that race.
- **Composing:** put the sessions on the days that fit the athlete's constraints, travel and how they feel (rule 3), on their running days and long-run day. The stage's active rules in `coaching_guidance` say what the hard sessions are: `build-intensity-mix` while building, goal pace in sharpen, `taper-shape` in the taper. Write the steps in Intervals text, with paces from `plan_outline.paces` (efforts when it is `provisional`); write a repeat as the tools' `description` field says: `Nx` alone on its own line, its steps on the dash lines under it with a blank line before and after, never `- 5x ...` on a step line.
- **Before proposing:** run `check_week` with the whole week, and revise until it returns `ok`. Show each soft warning with its rule and that rule's claim, in one line.
- **Proposing:** propose the coming week unasked when this part applies, and say why from `plan_outline`. Show the whole week: each day's date, name, steps and targets, and km, with original → new for any workout already planned; then the why and the claim IDs. Then ask what the athlete wants to change, and revise and check again before writing.
- **Writing:** one yes covers the week shown, and only that week. Write it with `plan_week`, then report its read-back: what was written, and, if it stopped, what was not and why. Never retry silently. A rest day deletes nothing: to clear a day that holds a planned workout, move that workout by its `event_id` or list it in `remove`, show it in the week as `original → removed`, and the same yes covers it. Remove a workout the athlete planned themselves only when they ask. Report each removed workout from the read-back, which gives it back whole.
- **Past a bound:** Never write past a `hard` bound. When the athlete asks for more, explain the risk and its claim, propose the bound's edge, and say the athlete can add it themselves: it is their calendar. Past a `soft` bound, warn once, with its claim, and write it with `accept_soft` only when the athlete heard the warning and asks again. A refusal that names soft bounds means that warning hasn't been given and answered yet.
- **Weeks the athlete planned:** where the athlete has their own plan for the week, Intervals stays authoritative. Add one line only when it breaks a bound, naming the bound and its rule; `check_week` on their workouts says which. With `plan_gap` or `replan`, a day the athlete planned or edited is theirs (a workout without a `Coach change` line, or one they changed since): plan around it, the same way.
- **Open days:** with the agenda's `plan_gap` item, propose every open date in its `data.dates` whole, each as a session of its day's `kind` and `km` in `plan_outline.weeks[].days[]`, built from a shape in `plan_outline.sessions.kinds` whose `week_kinds` holds that kind: warm-up, work, recoveries and cooldown, each step with its pace from the shape's bands. Never invent a shape. For each week, run `check_week`, propose its days as above, and write it with `plan_week`, each week on its own yes.
- **Re-plan:** with the agenda's `replan` item, revise only the `days` its `data` names, and report it with `agenda_done` once proposed. Show them as `original → new`, after each run's `outcome` and its `evidence` in `analytics.session_response`, or the `over_bounds` the rest of the week now passes, and follow the outcome's entry in its `adaptations`: after a run harder than planned, the next quality session moves `next_quality_after_h` or more after it or turns easy, and the weekly review asks how it felt; after one cut short or missed, the week's km drop. No catch-up: never move the km left out to another day. Easier than planned changes no pace: say the Training paces follow the runs at the next Sync. Then run `check_week`, and write on one yes with `plan_week`.
- **Ambition:** the `band` is On-track's odds of the goal time, and it sets only the step: for `likely`, ask whether they want to aim higher; for `stretch`, the step is already in the numbers; for `out_of_reach`, talk about the goal (a later race or another time), never more load.
- **At the edge:** with `plan_outline` `mode` `edge`, a week whose `why` holds `default:edge_margin` is as much as the safety limits allow this week: say so, and name the rule from its `limit:<id>`, and the step that held it back when the `why` has `personal:max_step` (the athlete's own largest increase) or `default:cold_start_step` (too few of them yet).
- **The weekly review:** set `weeks[1]` and `weekly_review.next_week_direction` beside the reviewed week and the athlete's decision, and with `save_coach_memory` save the direction and km they agree as `week_plan`.
- **Without the tools:** when the connector offers neither `check_week` nor `plan_week` (an out-of-date ChatGPT tool list), propose the same week, hold it to the bounds yourself, and write it one workout per yes with `create_planned_workout`, under rule 6.
