# Race times

This applies before any reply that quotes, compares or explains a range or the odds of a goal time from `analytics.race_prediction`: a race-time question, the check-in's ACTIVE-RACE SIGNAL, the weekly review, or the first chat after a race. It also applies before race-day heat advice from `goal_weather`, and to the agenda's `after_race`, `method_change` and `course` items.

`analytics.race_prediction` is the only source of a race time. With `status` `available` it holds the scenarios **Race scenarios** (below) says how to quote: ranges for standard distances (`distances`) and, when a goal race is set, for the goal (`goal`), each fitted to the athlete's own races and checked against how well the same method predicted their earlier races from data before each (`calibration`), or, while its `regime` is `cold_start`, from their training alone (**From training alone**, below).

- **Its range, never a single time:** Quote a time only as its range, `p10_s` to `p90_s` (an 80% range), with `p50_s` as the middle; never give a single time without its range, and never a time for a distance it does not list.
- **What it rests on:** With `regime` `raced`, say what it rests on: `basis.races_used` races from the last three years, and a backtest that predicted `calibration.races_backtested` of the athlete's races, older ones included, each from the races before it (the two counts are separate: never call the backtested races a share of the races used); when fewer than 4 were backtested, say the range leans on population data and is wide on purpose.
- **From training alone:** While `regime` is `cold_start`, they have no race the curve can use, and the range comes from their training alone. Say it is "from your training alone, no race yet; wide on purpose", and what its `champion` reads: `vdot-training-effort-v1`, the best effort of 1500 m to 10K in their runs of the last 90 days, by Daniels' VDOT; `training-calibrated-v1`, their recent training, on a model fitted to other runners' training and races. Give no race or backtest count for it. Its goal race has no On-track range (`goal.reason` `needs_a_race`: no range from training alone was checked that far ahead): give Rested at the goal's distance as of today, say it is not a prediction for race day, and that a race of their own brings one. In the same reply, ask about `analytics.race_candidates` as `references/race-questions.md` says, at most once a chat: one confirmed race gives a range fitted to their own races from the next Sync. With none to ask about, say a race or an all-out time trial would do the same.
- **Without a range:** With `status` `unavailable`, give its `reason` and estimate no time any other way: `too_little_training`, too little recent running for a range from training alone (`vdot-training-effort-v1` needs a best effort of 1500 m to 10K in the last 90 days), which their next weeks of running can give; `unavailable_this_sync`, the range could not be worked out in this Sync, which the next one should bring back; `no_maximal_race`, a release that gives no range from training alone, so only a confirmed race or an all-out time trial gives one. Offer `analytics.race_candidates` as `references/race-questions.md` says.
- **The odds of a goal time:** Give the odds of a time only from the goal's quantiles: `goal.p_goal` when the goal time from `get_athlete_profile` (or `race_context.active_goal`) equals `goal.goal_time_s`; otherwise interpolate between the two published quantiles around it, saying under 5% below `p05_s` and over 95% above `p95_s`, never more precisely. These odds are measured from the athlete's own backtest, not your confidence (rule 4).
- **What it assumes:** Name what the range assumes: `goal.course` (`course_activity` or `elevation_given`: the course's climbs and descents on a published grade curve; `assumed_flat`: a flat course, and `hills_only_slower` says hills on it can only make the range slower), `goal.weather.source` (`none`: race-day weather is not in it; `climatology`: the usual weather for that date and hour; `forecast`: today's forecast, which changes), whose heat cost, `goal.weather.heat_model` `published`, comes from a published curve by distance and finish time (`env-heat-optimum-falls-with-distance`; `weather_factor` is the slowing it adds), never `analytics.heat`, and what On-track carries forward (**Race scenarios**), never a promise that training will make it faster.
- **Flags:** Say each of `goal.flags`, and a distance's `extrapolation` of `marathon_prior` (no race of 25 km or more, so its range follows population data).
- **Race-day weather without a range:** When a goal is set but `goal` does not price it (the section is `unavailable`, or a `goal.reason` such as `beyond_evidence`), `goal_weather` is still the race day's weather, `source` as for `goal.weather`, over the goal time or an assumed window: use it for heat advice, never for a time.

**The course question.** The agenda's `course` item (`data.goal_date`) is due while the goal race's `goal.course` is `assumed_flat`. Ask once for the course, after what the athlete came for and anything urgent: a run of theirs on it, or its gain and loss (the organiser's course profile gives them). Say the range is for a flat course, and that hills can only slow it, never speed it up.

- **Net downhill:** When they don't know the gain and loss, ask whether the course is net downhill, and by how much: a point-to-point course that finishes lower than it starts is the one way it can run faster than flat.
- **Saving it:** Save what they give with `set_goal_race`: `course_activity_id`, or `elevation_gain_m` and `elevation_loss_m`, or for a net-downhill course its drop alone as `elevation_loss_m`; it counts from the next Sync, and the save closes the item. If they don't know or let it pass, report it with `agenda_done`.
- **Without the item:** ask about the course only when the athlete asks for a race time for the goal race.

**Race scenarios.** `analytics.race_prediction.scenarios` names, for each scenario, its `champion` (the model whose range it is), its `regime` (`raced`, or `cold_start`: no race of their own to predict from) and its `range`, the field that holds it: Race-today (`race_today`: racing as they are now, fatigue included) and Rested (`rested`: starting a taper today and racing when it ends) for each standard distance as of today (`distances`), Rested in `rested_distances` when its range is its own, and On-track (`on_track`: training on as lately, staying healthy and tapering into the goal race) for the goal race on its date (`goal`), null without a goal race the section prices. Quote one scenario per question, the one matching what the athlete will do before the race, under the rules above for every range:

| Situation | Quote |
|---|---|
| A goal race is set | On-track, for the goal race on its date |
| No goal race | Rested, at the standard distance asked |
| A race this week that isn't the goal race | Race-today, with Rested beside it |
| Tracking fitness | Rested, week over week |
| A race further out that isn't the goal race | Rested as of today: say you don't predict that date, and never adjust the range to reach it |
| A cold-start athlete | the same rules, with the basis named; for the goal race, Rested at its distance (`needs_a_race`) |
| A week or more without a run (`no_recent_runs`) | no On-track: Rested at the goal's distance, saying On-track returns with their next run |

- **On-track's wording:** More than 7 days out (`goal.days_to_race`), lead with the condition, what `on_track.assumptions` carries forward: the recent training (`recent_training`, over its `days`), `weekly_km` a week, a long run of `long_run_km` and `quality_sessions_per_week` quality sessions a week (runs whose heart rate reached 90% of their peak; `runs_without_heart_rate` runs could not be counted, and null means none could); the taper (`taper`), its `days` days at no more than `volume_share_max` of their usual running; and staying healthy (`stays_healthy`). Then say the range is wide for the weeks still to go. Within 7 days, lead with the range and the odds of the goal time. While `assumptions.training_gain_pct` is 0, say the range "credits no improvement from the training still to come", and that it doesn't credit the build in their plan.
- **A disrupted block:** when `on_track.disruption.disrupted` is true, a stop of a week or more (`longest_stop_days`) came between 84 and 22 days before the goal race. A gap still going on reaches a week as `no_recent_runs` instead. Say the range assumes training resumes as before and hasn't been tested through a disruption like this, and give `disruption.rested_at_goal_distance` beside it: Rested at the goal's distance, as of today. The flag clears when a later Sync no longer finds the stop.
- **Gaps between scenarios:** name a gap between two scenarios only when one model fills both (the same `champion`) and it credits a gain (`taper_gain_pct` or `training_gain_pct` above 0): Race-today against Rested is the fatigue a taper would shed. Otherwise quote them side by side, never their difference as fatigue or fitness. While Race-today and Rested read the same `range`, they are one range: no taper gain is credited (`taper_gain_pct` 0), so never name a gap between them or say a taper would make them faster. When one model fills Rested and On-track, Rested's change is the fitness part of On-track's move.
- **A race that isn't the goal race:** in the week ahead, Race-today with Rested beside it, so a race mid-block is no verdict on their fitness; while they are one range, say so. Further out, Rested as of today: say you don't predict that date, and never adjust the range to reach it.
- **After a race:** the agenda's `after_race` item (`data.activity_id`, `data.date`, `data.goal_race`) is the first chat after a race; report it with `agenda_done` once its result is set beside the quotes. `analytics.race_prediction.after_race` lists the confirmed road races of the last 84 days, newest first (at most 4: the goal race always, then the newest others), each beside what was quoted before it (`compared`), as the Sync kept it and never worked out again. In that chat, set its result (`time_s`) beside the quote of the scenario that matches what the athlete did, and say which scenario it was: the goal race (`goal_race`) beside On-track as quoted 84, 28, 7 and 1 days before it; any other race by its `taper`, `untapered` beside Race-today at 7 and 1 days, `tapered` beside Rested at the taper's start (7 days before, 14 before a half or marathon), and `ambiguous` beside both, said to be between the two. Give each quote as its range, `p10_s` to `p90_s`, and where the result fell in it (`quantile`: 0.5 is the middle, under 0.1 or over 0.9 outside the 80% range). Race-today and Rested are for a flat course, so name a hilly course. A `horizon_days` longer than the one named means no Sync ran that day and the latest from up to 2 days earlier stands in. An empty `compared` means no quote was kept from before it: say so, and never work one out after the race. One race is no verdict on their fitness.
- **A change of method:** when a scenario carries `change`, its method changed on `change.date`, from `change.from` to the scenario's `champion` (to `fallback.champion` during a fallback). Say so once, when the agenda's `method_change` item (`data.date`, `data.scenarios`) is served, and how the range moved: set `change.before`, the range quoted before it (On-track's `p10_s`, `p50_s` and `p90_s`, else the middle, `p50_s`, by standard distance), beside today's. Say why, by `change.why`: `cleared`, a method checked on other runners' races that ran beside the old one for two weeks without their own races showing it worse; `harm_shown`, their own races showed the newer method predicting them worse, so the earlier one is back; `release`, a release changed it for everyone. Report the item with `agenda_done` once said. While a scenario's `champion` is not `race-prediction-v1`, `basis` and `calibration` describe v1's curve, not its range: name the model whose range it is instead, and give no backtest count for it. `analytics.race_champions` is the Sync's record of which model each scenario quotes, any model running in shadow beside it, and their checks, for whoever runs the hub: never quote a model in shadow or the checks' numbers.
- **A fallback:** when a scenario carries `fallback`, its champion (`fallback.champion`) failed in this Sync, so the range is the one it replaced (`champion`): quote it as usual, and say it is the earlier method's this time; never estimate the other's.
- **Course and weather turned off:** when `goal.flags` holds `course_and_weather_off`, their own goal races showed the published course and weather terms made the range worse for them, so it leaves them out: say the range is for a flat course in neutral weather, still naming the course and weather as `goal.course` and `goal.weather` give them, and that their own races turned the terms off.
- **In the weekly review:** When `analytics.race_prediction.scenarios.on_track` is present, set On-track's range and odds beside `previous_week.goal`'s, last week's quote as the Sync kept it, and say which input moved them: `previous_week.goal.moved_s` splits the middle's move into `fitness` (a new race, or the fitness since), the `course` and the `weather`, and `champion_changed` means the method changed; the weeks to go only widen the range. Give Rested's change at the goal's distance (`previous_week.goal.rested_moved_s`: this week's middle less last week's, at the goal's own distance, a standard one or between two) as the fitness part. When `previous_week` is null, no quote was kept from a week ago: give this week's alone. With no goal race, give Rested week over week (`distances` against `previous_week.distances`), at the last goal race's distance, else the most-raced standard distance, else 10K.
