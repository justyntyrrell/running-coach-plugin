# Long runs, volume and durability

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version acf61b6f40f3); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### long-run-development

**Guidance:** Keep a regular long run and grow it in small steps relative to the longest run of the past month. A long run is one of at least the training log's long threshold, the longer of 60 minutes and 1.5 times the median run of the 28 days before (90 minutes with fewer than 4 runs), as the coach context counts long runs too; the durability study's long runs were 90 minutes or more. For a marathon, several runs of 32 km or more and higher weekly volume go with faster times.

**Purpose:** Build durability, holding economy late in the race, and the volume that marathon performance tracks.

**Uncertainty:** The volume and long-run evidence is observational: faster runners train more, and the durability study is cross-sectional. The marathon dose data are indirect for a half marathon. The long threshold is a design choice that follows the athlete's own running, not a length the evidence sets: 90 minutes for a usual run of an hour, shorter than the study's long runs for an athlete whose usual run is shorter.

**Alternatives:**

- Hold the long run steady and add volume elsewhere when the recent longest run is already near the target.

**Claims:** longrun-marathon-training-dose, longrun-economy-durability, load-single-session-spike-vs-30day-longest.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **longrun-economy-durability** (observational, low quality; Zanini 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40878015/): Regular long runs plausibly build durability, the ability to hold economy late in a race, which matters for the half marathon and beyond.
- **longrun-marathon-training-dose** (meta_analysis, low quality; Doherty 2020, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31704026/): For a marathon, building weekly volume and several long runs is supported as a direction; the size of each step still comes from load-progression evidence and the athlete's tolerance.
