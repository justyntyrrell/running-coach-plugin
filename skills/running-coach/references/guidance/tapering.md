# Tapering and peaking before a race

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version acf61b6f40f3); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### taper-shape

**Guidance:** Taper by cutting volume progressively, to roughly 40-60% below usual by race week, while keeping race-relevant intensity and nearly all run days. Take volume out before quality, and avoid a rebound week.

**Purpose:** Shed accumulated fatigue while keeping the fitness already built. Expect freshness, not new fitness.

**Uncertainty:** The pooled evidence comes from small studies across endurance sports. The one half-marathon trial (a 7-day, 85% cut) found no gain, and the best taper length differs by source. Expect a gain of a few percent at most, varying by athlete. The rule does not wait for a pain answer: pain or illness the athlete reports overrides it.

**Alternatives:**

- A shorter 7-10 day taper when the preceding weeks were not heavy.
- A deeper volume cut when fatigue or niggles are high, accepting that very deep cuts were not better on average.

**Claims:** taper-wang-endurance-components, taper-bosquet-2wk-volume-cut, taper-keep-run-frequency, taper-keep-intense-running-short-taper, taper-strict-consecutive-2-3wk-recreational, taper-elite-distance-runner-practice, taper-half-marathon-deep-short-null, taper-expected-gain-about-3pct.

### taper-plan

**Guidance:** Plan the taper now: when the last long run and the last hard session fall, and how far volume comes down in each of the final two weeks. Keep the final weeks falling in consecutive steps.

**Purpose:** A taper decided in advance avoids a late rebound week or a crash block.

**Uncertainty:** Evidence on taper length is mixed: about two weeks in pooled studies, three weeks in recreational marathoners, about a week in elite track runners.

**Alternatives:**

- Start the reduction at 10 days rather than 14 when recent training has been lighter.

**Claims:** taper-wang-endurance-components, taper-bosquet-2wk-volume-cut, taper-strict-consecutive-2-3wk-recreational, taper-elite-distance-runner-practice.

### taper-no-late-overload

**Guidance:** Do not add extra load late to bank fitness. If key-session performance is falling while fatigue is high, reduce load rather than push through.

**Purpose:** Heavy training before a taper helped only athletes who were acutely fatigued, not those who were overreached.

**Uncertainty:** A single small study in triathletes; whether an athlete is overreached is clear only in hindsight.

**Alternatives:**

- Keep the planned block unchanged when key sessions are holding and recovery signals are normal.

**Claims:** taper-overload-only-without-overreaching.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **taper-bosquet-2wk-volume-cut** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Bosquet 2007, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/): Default to a taper of about 10-14 days: bring weekly volume down progressively to roughly 40-60% below normal, keep quality at race-relevant intensity and keep the number of run days. It is a volume cut, not a load cut, so a 14-day load ratio stays above the volume ratio when intensity is kept.
- **taper-elite-distance-runner-practice** (elite_practice, very_low quality; Spilsbury 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25189116/): A 7-14 day taper that keeps short, faster-than-race-pace interval work fits elite practice for a half marathon, which sits between those groups. It shows what elites do, not that it works.
- **taper-expected-gain-about-3pct** (narrative_review, low quality; Mujika 2003, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12840640/): Expect a few percent at most and wide individual variation; treat the taper as removing fatigue and do not over-read one race.
- **taper-gain-8km-cross-country-race** (observational, low quality; Luden 2010, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20299622/): Expect a taper before a 5-10 km race to be worth about 3% against racing in normal training, as freshness rather than new aerobic fitness. It is one small study without a control group, so read it beside the pooled trials.
- **taper-gain-spread-olympic-swimmers** (observational, low quality; Mujika 2002, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12439774/): Treat a taper's gain as a range rather than a fixed bonus: about 1.5 points either side of the average between athletes, and a few get slower. Never promise one athlete the average.
- **taper-half-marathon-deep-short-null** (rct, low quality; Child 2000, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10950440/): Do not assume a very deep, very short cut improves a half marathon; direct evidence is thin and null, and 85% is beyond the 41-60% linked to the best pooled effects.
- **taper-keep-intense-running-short-taper** (rct, low quality; Houmard 1994, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8007812/): Keep race-specific high-intensity running inside the taper rather than swapping it for cross-training.
- **taper-keep-run-frequency** (rct, low quality; Mujika 2002, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12165889/): Shorten runs rather than removing run days through the taper and race week; this agrees with the pooled frequency findings.
- **taper-overload-only-without-overreaching** (rct, low quality; Aubry 2014, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25134000/): Do not add a late crash block to bank fitness unless key-session performance is holding; if performance falls while fatigue is high, reduce load instead. Most peaks came in the first two weeks of the taper.
- **taper-strict-consecutive-2-3wk-recreational** (observational, low quality; Smyth 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34651125/): Make the taper monotonic, each final week lower than the one before with no rebound week; do not copy the marathon-derived 3-week length to a half marathon directly.
- **taper-wang-endurance-components** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Wang 2023, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37163550/): Keep intensity and frequency and cut volume by about 40-60%; smaller and larger cuts were not significant. Any duration up to 21 days worked. Expect freshness, not new fitness: VO2max and economy did not change.
