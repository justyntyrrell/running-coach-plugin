# Intensity distribution, periodization, threshold, VO2max and race-specific work

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version acf61b6f40f3); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### build-intensity-mix

**Guidance:** Outside the final four weeks, keep most running easy (easy_pct_4w is the training log's measured share) and build around a pyramidal or polarized mix: regular threshold-zone work, and some work above threshold such as 3-6 minute intervals near VO2max in each block. Anchor race-specific sessions to goal pace. Do not chase a distribution label.

**Purpose:** Raise aerobic power and threshold while protecting the easy volume that best predicts distance-running performance.

**Uncertainty:** Polarized and pyramidal models perform about the same on average; the trials are short and small, and elite practice shows what the best do, not what causes their results. Some world-class runners do well with threshold-heavy weeks. The rule is not gated on the measured mix: none of its claims reports a zone share as a finding, and the log splits time at 0.88 and 1.00 of threshold pace, not at the ventilatory or lactate thresholds the studies used.

**Alternatives:**

- A short polarized block when VO2max is the limiter, accepting no clear race benefit.
- Several controlled sub-threshold interval sessions a week for a high-volume athlete who recovers well from them.

**Claims:** intensity-polarized-pyramidal-equivalent, intensity-polarized-small-vo2peak-edge, intensity-elite-pyramidal-to-polarized, intensity-threshold-dominant-model-less-effective, intensity-above-mmss-raises-vo2peak, intensity-long-intervals-over-sprints, intensity-easy-volume-predicts-elite-performance, strides-hiit-beats-sit-for-vo2max.

### quality-density

**Guidance:** Three or more hard days in the last completed week (Monday to Sunday): days the training log labelled a race, intervals, threshold or hills from the runs' own samples. Check that the days between them were easy (b2b counts back-to-back hard days) and that the next hard day earns its place; in a trained runner more interval dose adds little.

**Purpose:** Keep the hard-easy pattern that elite and pyramidal models share, and avoid paying fatigue for diminishing returns.

**Uncertainty:** No trial sets a safe number of hard days a week: 3 is a coach choice, and some world-class models use four or five tightly controlled sessions. The log labels a run from its samples against the athlete's own threshold, so a hard run counts whatever its name, races included; a low-confidence label is a question for the athlete, not a fact.

**Alternatives:**

- Keep the week unchanged when one of the sessions was a controlled sub-threshold workout and recovery signals are normal.

**Claims:** intensity-interval-dose-small-in-trained, intensity-elite-pyramidal-to-polarized, intensity-threshold-intervals-elite-model, intensity-easy-volume-predicts-elite-performance, recovery-no-single-overtraining-marker.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **intensity-above-mmss-raises-vo2peak** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Rosenblat 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37737543/): Include some work above threshold (VO2max-type intervals) in each build block; do not expect it alone to move race times in a few weeks.
- **intensity-contraception-oc-performance-trivial-difference** (meta_analysis, low quality; Elliott-Sale 2020, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32666247/): Any average effect of the pill on performance is likely trivial: do not plan around it, and do not schedule key sessions or races around her pill-free days. If a pill user feels her performance varies, judge it from her own training data and symptoms rather than from a general rule.
- **intensity-contraception-oc-vo2max-no-difference** (meta_analysis, low quality; Schumpf 2023, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37484400/): Do not adjust VO2max-based paces or fitness expectations for pill use or pill phase. A change of a couple of ml/kg/min after starting the pill cannot be ruled out, so retest fitness rather than assume it is unchanged.
- **intensity-easy-volume-predicts-elite-performance** (observational, low quality; Casado 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31045681/): Easy volume is not filler: protect it, and add tempo and short-interval work to it rather than replacing easy running with more intensity.
- **intensity-elite-pyramidal-to-polarized** (elite_practice, very_low quality; Casado 2022, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35418513/): For a half-marathon or marathon build, a pyramidal base (much easy running, regular threshold work, some faster work) with hard-easy days is consistent with elite practice.
- **intensity-hiit-vs-continuous-vo2max-untrained** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Milanović 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26243014/): Evidence that intervals are efficient for VO2max comes largely from untrained people; for a trained runner it supports including intervals, not replacing easy volume.
- **intensity-interval-dose-small-in-trained** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Rosenblat 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33826121/): For a trained runner, more or harder interval sessions give diminishing returns; spend quality where it is specific and recoverable rather than stacking more.
- **intensity-long-intervals-over-sprints** (meta_analysis, low quality; Rosenblat 2020, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32034701/): For distance running, favour longer aerobic intervals (about 3-6 min near VO2max or slightly below) over all-out sprints as the main above-threshold stimulus.
- **intensity-polarized-pyramidal-equivalent** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Rosenblat 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39888556/): Do not treat the label (polarized or pyramidal) as the lever. Keep most running easy and choose the distribution that fits the athlete and event; recreational runners may do slightly better on pyramidal, competitive ones on polarized.
- **intensity-polarized-small-vo2peak-edge** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Silva Oliveira 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38717713/): A polarized block may be a reasonable short-term choice to lift aerobic power, but expect no extra race-performance gain from the distribution alone.
- **intensity-threshold-dominant-model-less-effective** (systematic_review, low quality; Kenneally 2018, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29182410/): Avoid a block where most quality sits at threshold with little easy running and nothing faster. Anchoring sessions to goal race pace is a reasonable way to design race-specific work.
- **intensity-threshold-intervals-elite-model** (elite_practice, very_low quality; Casado 2023, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36900796/): Frequent sub-threshold interval work can be a legitimate option for a high-volume athlete if intensity is tightly controlled; it is not proof that more threshold sessions are better for everyone.
