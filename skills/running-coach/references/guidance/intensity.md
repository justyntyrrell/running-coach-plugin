# Intensity distribution, periodization, threshold, VO2max and race-specific work

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version a596fb349f63); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### build-intensity-mix

**Guidance:** Outside the final four weeks, keep most running easy (easy_pct_4w is the training log's measured share) and build around a pyramidal or polarized mix: regular threshold-zone work, and some work above threshold such as 3-6 minute intervals near VO2max in each block. Anchor race-specific sessions to goal pace. Do not chase a distribution label.

**Purpose:** Raise aerobic power and threshold while protecting the easy volume that best predicts distance-running performance.

**Uncertainty:** Polarized and pyramidal models perform about the same on average; the trials are short and small, and elite practice shows what the best do, not what causes their results. Some world-class runners do well with threshold-heavy weeks. The rule is not gated on the measured mix: none of its claims reports a zone share as a finding, and the log splits time at 0.88 and 1.00 of threshold pace, not at the ventilatory or lactate thresholds the studies used.

**Alternatives:**

- A short polarized block when VO2max is the limiter, accepting no clear race benefit.
- Several controlled sub-threshold interval sessions a week for a high-volume athlete who recovers well from them.

**Claims:** intensity-polarized-pyramidal-equivalent, intensity-polarized-small-vo2peak-edge, intensity-elite-pyramidal-to-polarized, intensity-threshold-dominant-model-less-effective, intensity-above-mmss-raises-vo2peak, intensity-long-intervals-over-sprints, intensity-easy-volume-predicts-elite-performance, strides-hiit-beats-sit-for-vo2max.

### quality-density

**Guidance:** Three or more hard days in the last completed week (Monday to Sunday): days the training log labelled a race, intervals, threshold or hills from the runs' own samples. Check that the days between them were easy (b2b counts back-to-back hard days) and that the next hard day earns its place; in a trained runner more interval dose adds little.

**Purpose:** Keep the hard-easy pattern that elite and pyramidal models share, and avoid paying fatigue for diminishing returns.

**Uncertainty:** No trial sets a safe number of hard days a week: 3 is a coach choice, and some world-class models use four or five tightly controlled sessions. The log labels a run from its samples against the athlete's own threshold, so a hard run counts whatever its name, races included; a low-confidence label is a question for the athlete, not a fact.

**Alternatives:**

- Keep the week unchanged when one of the sessions was a controlled sub-threshold workout and recovery signals are normal.

**Claims:** intensity-interval-dose-small-in-trained, intensity-elite-pyramidal-to-polarized, intensity-threshold-intervals-elite-model, intensity-easy-volume-predicts-elite-performance, recovery-no-single-overtraining-marker.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **intensity-30-30-active-recovery-raised-vo2max-7wk** (rct, low quality; Ben Abderrahman 2013, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23229881/): A block of 30/30 sessions can lift vVO2max and endurance at it within 7 weeks, and with a jogged recovery it also lifted VO2max in this small trial. If using 30/30s, jog the 30 s off; the abstract gives no direct test of active against passive recovery, so do not present one as proven better.
- **intensity-30-30-at-105-vvo2max-doubles-time-above-90** (observational, low quality; Millet 2003, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12811566/): If a runner does 30/30s, run the fast 30 s about 5% faster than vVO2max rather than at it; at exactly vVO2max, VO2 peaked well below its maximum (66 vs 71 ml/kg/min) in these 30 s reps. Runners whose VO2 rises slowly gain most.
- **intensity-30-30-hr-overstates-time-near-vo2max** (observational, low quality; Fleckenstein 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39835194/): Do not judge a 30/30 session by time spent above 90% of max heart rate: in short reps heart rate stays high while oxygen uptake does not, so heart rate overstates time near VO2max. If time near VO2max is the goal, prefer reps of about 3 minutes over faster 30/30s.
- **intensity-30-30-more-time-at-vo2max-than-continuous** (observational, low quality; Billat 2000, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10638376/): Against a hard continuous run between threshold and vVO2max, a 30/30 at vVO2max with a jogged half-speed recovery lets a runner spend more time at VO2max; it is a usable entry to VO2max work. The spread between runners was very large, and the comparison was with a continuous run, not with longer reps.
- **intensity-4x4-intervals-beat-equal-work-moderate** (rct, moderate quality; Helgerud 2007, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17414804/): When the goal of a block is VO2max specifically, a weekly session of long hard intervals (4 x 4 min, or 15/15) does more than the same minutes at threshold or easy. It is one 8-week study in men with no easy-volume base being varied, so it argues for including the session, not for making the whole week hard.
- **intensity-60-30-beats-30-30-time-above-90-vo2max** (observational, low quality; Millet 2003, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12955868/): At the same running speed and the same total fast time, lengthening the rep from 30 s to 60 s (or to half of time to exhaustion) with short recoveries roughly triples time above 90% VO2max; a 30/30 at vVO2max is the weaker VO2max stimulus of the two.
- **intensity-above-mmss-raises-vo2peak** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Rosenblat 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37737543/): Include some work above threshold (VO2max-type intervals) in each build block; do not expect it alone to move race times in a few weeks.
- **intensity-blood-volume-carries-the-cardiac-gain** (observational, low quality; Bonne 2014, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24622974/): Early VO2max gains run largely through more circulating blood rather than a remodelled heart, which is one reason a few weeks of consistent aerobic work can move VO2max. Treat it as mechanism from untrained adults: it says nothing about which session to run.
- **intensity-easy-volume-predicts-elite-performance** (observational, low quality; Casado 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31045681/): Easy volume is not filler: protect it, and add tempo and short-interval work to it rather than replacing easy running with more intensity.
- **intensity-elite-pyramidal-to-polarized** (elite_practice, very_low quality; Casado 2022, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35418513/): For a half-marathon or marathon build, a pyramidal base (much easy running, regular threshold work, some faster work) with hard-easy days is consistent with elite practice.
- **intensity-hiit-central-sit-peripheral** (meta_analysis, low quality; Rosenblat 2022, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35041180/): Treat the two as different jobs: longer hard intervals are the session that builds the heart's pumping side, short all-out sprints mainly work the muscle. For a distance runner chasing VO2max, make sustained intervals the staple and use sprints for other reasons, not as a swap.
- **intensity-hiit-vs-continuous-vo2max-untrained** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Milanović 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26243014/): Evidence that intervals are efficient for VO2max comes largely from untrained people; for a trained runner it supports including intervals, not replacing easy volume.
- **intensity-interval-dose-small-in-trained** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Rosenblat 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33826121/): For a trained runner, more or harder interval sessions give diminishing returns; spend quality where it is specific and recoverable rather than stacking more.
- **intensity-long-intervals-over-sprints** (meta_analysis, low quality; Rosenblat 2020, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32034701/): For distance running, favour longer aerobic intervals (about 3-6 min near VO2max or slightly below) over all-out sprints as the main above-threshold stimulus.
- **intensity-long-reps-most-time-near-vo2max** (meta_analysis, low quality; Schoenmakers 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42237396/): When the aim of a session is time near VO2max, build it from reps of 2 minutes or more (about 3-5 minutes for a distance runner) rather than 30/30s or 1-minute reps; jog or stand between reps as the runner prefers. Treat it as a guide to session design, not proof of bigger fitness gains: the review did not link time near VO2max to long-term adaptation.
- **intensity-moderate-domain-no-vo2max-gain-6wk** (rct, moderate quality; Inglis 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38376995/): Easy running below the first threshold is not what lifts VO2max or threshold over a few weeks; a build needs some work above it. Keep easy running for what it is for (volume, durability, recovery between hard days), and do not tell an Athlete their easy runs are wasted: this trial measured VO2max and thresholds in non-runners over six weeks, not race performance.
- **intensity-oxygen-delivery-limits-vo2max** (narrative_review, low quality; Bassett DR 2000, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10647532/): Frame the two sides of training separately for an Athlete: work that stresses the delivery system raises the ceiling, while aerobic volume and its muscle adaptations raise the fraction of that ceiling they can hold. When judging fitness, weight threshold pace over VO2max, since the review names threshold speed as the better predictor of race performance.
- **intensity-polarized-pyramidal-equivalent** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Rosenblat 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39888556/): Do not treat the label (polarized or pyramidal) as the lever. Keep most running easy and choose the distribution that fits the athlete and event; recreational runners may do slightly better on pyramidal, competitive ones on polarized.
- **intensity-polarized-small-vo2peak-edge** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Silva Oliveira 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38717713/): A polarized block may be a reasonable short-term choice to lift aerobic power, but expect no extra race-performance gain from the distribution alone.
- **intensity-session-duration-dose-response-vo2max** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Mattioni Maturana 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42730846/): For VO2max, a longer interval session adds little past about 20-25 minutes including recoveries, so extra time is better spent on steady aerobic volume than on more reps. For a time-short Athlete, a short session that is truly hard does more for VO2max than the same minutes easy. The numbers are averages from untrained people with wide intervals; do not quote 11 minutes to a trained runner as a target.
- **intensity-threshold-dominant-model-less-effective** (systematic_review, low quality; Kenneally 2018, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29182410/): Avoid a block where most quality sits at threshold with little easy running and nothing faster. Anchoring sessions to goal race pace is a reasonable way to design race-specific work.
- **intensity-threshold-intervals-elite-model** (elite_practice, very_low quality; Casado 2023, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36900796/): Frequent sub-threshold interval work can be a legitimate option for a high-volume athlete if intensity is tightly controlled; it is not proof that more threshold sessions are better for everyone.
- **intensity-untrained-muscle-oxidative-gain-outruns-vo2max** (observational, very_low quality; Zoladz 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42976123/): A reminder that the muscle and the whole body improve at different rates: threshold power can move far more than VO2max over a block, so judge a build by threshold and race-pace work as well as by VO2max. Untrained men on a bike for seven weeks with no control group, so the sizes do not transfer to a trained runner.
- **intensity-volume-builds-mitochondria-intensity-their-function** (narrative_review, low quality; Granata 2018, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29934848/): Easy and steady volume is what grows mitochondrial mass; harder work is what sharpens their function. That is an argument for keeping both in a week rather than trading volume for intensity, but it is a review of muscle measures, not of race results, so do not quote it as a reason to set any particular weekly mileage.
