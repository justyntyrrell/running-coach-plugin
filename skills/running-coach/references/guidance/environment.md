# Heat and altitude

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version acf61b6f40f3); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### heat-response

**Guidance:** The latest run, or the goal race's forecast, sits in the warm (air above 15 C) or humid (dew point above 12 C) band where the athlete's own heat model starts counting a heart-rate cost. Heart rate climbs at a steady pace in the heat, partly from fluid loss, and drinking blunts some of the rise. Judge effort by heart rate and perceived effort rather than pace, start hydrated, and expect slower paces, by about what personal_evidence says heat costs this athlete. If the race may be warm, 1-2 weeks of repeated warm-weather training beforehand is the main preparation, and goal pace should be adjusted on the day.

**Purpose:** Heat raises strain and slows endurance performance; acclimatisation is the best-supported countermeasure.

**Uncertainty:** 15 C and a 12 C dew point are where the athlete's heat model (analytics.heat) starts counting cost: knots of that model, design choices rather than evidence thresholds. No cited claim sets a temperature, and most study exercise in the heat (the dehydration review above 26.5 C), so at the mild end of the band the athlete's own cost says more than they do. Both weathers are modelled, without sun or wind; the race's counts only from its forecast, within 14 days of it. The athlete's own cost is an N=1 fit of heart rate, quoted as theirs only when its verdict is supported.

**Alternatives:**

- Move quality sessions to cooler times of day instead of adjusting their targets.
- Further out than the forecast, analytics.race_prediction.goal.weather, or goal_weather for a goal the prediction does not price, gives the race date's climatology: use it to decide early whether to plan 1-2 weeks of warm-weather training.

**Claims:** env-heat-acclimatise-1-2-weeks, env-heat-acclimation-improves-performance, env-marathon-slows-with-heat, hr-drift-heat-dehydration-relative-intensity, hr-dehydration-3bpm-per-percent-heat, hr-fluid-intake-lowers-exercise-hr.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **env-altitude-raises-haemoglobin-mass** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Gore 2013, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24282204/): A camp needs roughly two weeks or more (about 300 hours) of exposure to expect a haematological gain, and the gain fades within a few weeks.
- **env-heat-acclimation-improves-performance** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Benjamin 2019, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31827444/): Heat training is a real performance lever for hot races and may help modestly in cool conditions; plan it with the training-recovery balance in mind.
- **env-heat-acclimatise-1-2-weeks** (consensus, moderate quality; Racinais 2015, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26069301/): If a race may be hot, arrange about 1-2 weeks of repeated training in warm conditions beforehand; start sessions and races hydrated.
- **env-heat-optimum-falls-with-distance** (observational, low quality; Mantzios 2022, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34652333/): Expect a warm race day to cost time above an optimum that is lower the longer the race; this curve sizes the heat term in the goal race's published range.
- **env-intermittent-hypoxic-training-no-benefit** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Dorelli 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40548904/): Do not recommend altitude masks or brief hypoxic sessions as a way to boost fitness.
- **env-live-high-train-low-best** (meta_analysis, low quality; Bonetti 2009, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19203133/): Altitude camps are a possible lever for a planned block; natural live-high train-low has the best support, and gains are a few percent at most.
- **env-marathon-heat-cost-grows-with-finish-time** (observational, low quality; Knechtle 2019, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30849085/): Scale a heat cost up for slower finish times: a 4-hour marathoner loses about three times the share of their time per degree that a winner does.
- **env-marathon-slows-with-altitude** (observational, low quality; Lara 2014, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24444110/): A goal marathon at altitude will be markedly slower than at sea level; the published range flags altitude and does not price it.
- **env-marathon-slows-with-heat** (observational, low quality; Ely 2007, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17473775/): Adjust goal pace for warm race-day conditions rather than holding a cool-weather target; slower runners lose more.
- **env-mass-marathon-slows-above-12c** (observational, low quality; Vernon 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34444173/): For a 4:20-4:50 marathoner, expect roughly half a percent of finish time per degree above about 12 C.
