# Recovery after a half marathon or marathon

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version acf61b6f40f3); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### post-race-half-first-days

**Guidance:** The half marathon was in the last three days. Keep them to rest or short, easy running, with no hard session or race. Leg strength was back within two days in one study, probably of a treadmill half marathon; a road race may take longer, so bring intensity back once soreness has settled.

**Purpose:** Let race muscle damage settle before loading it again, without treating easy running as harmful.

**Uncertainty:** One uncontrolled study with a strength surrogate, very likely of a treadmill run, puts strength back within two days; the easy-running trial was in marathoners; in one small treadmill trial a second half marathon 7 days after the first was not slower. The 3 days are a coach choice, a day of margin for a road race: no study tested when hard training can resume.

**Alternatives:**

- A short easy run from day 2 when the athlete feels recovered and is not sore.

**Claims:** postrace-half-strength-back-1-2-days, postrace-easy-running-from-48h-markers-null, taper-half-marathon-deep-short-null.

### post-race-marathon-first-week

**Guidance:** The marathon was in the last week. Schedule no hard session or race this week; rest or short easy running are both reasonable. Muscle-damage markers took about a week to clear and leg strength could still be down at day 7, while lab-measured aerobic capacity was close to pre-race, so bring intensity back gradually and let soreness lead.

**Purpose:** Recover from marathon muscle damage before hard training, without fearing that an easy week costs fitness.

**Uncertainty:** Small trials and cohorts with surrogate outcomes: no study tested when hard training or another race can safely resume. The two recovery-week trials point different ways on easy running: moderate 40-minute runs every other day from 48 h showed no detectable difference from rest in blood markers, and in a tiny trial daily runs of up to 45 minutes slowed strength recovery. The 7 days are a coach choice.

**Alternatives:**

- Complete rest for the first 2-3 days when soreness is marked or the race was a hard personal best.
- A longer easy block into week two after a hot, hilly or unusually hard race.

**Claims:** postrace-marathon-damage-markers-week, postrace-marathon-strength-rest-week, postrace-easy-running-from-48h-markers-null, postrace-marathon-aerobic-capacity-7-days.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **postrace-easy-running-from-48h-markers-null** (rct, low quality; Martínez-Navarro 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33251988/): From about 48 h after a marathon, a moderate 40-minute run at about first-threshold heart rate (95-105% of the HR at VT1) every other day is a reasonable option: in one trial blood-marker recovery did not differ detectably from rest and jump recovery was better at 96 h. It does not support hard sessions in the first week.
- **postrace-half-strength-back-1-2-days** (observational, low quality; Wang 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40186759/): After a half marathon (here probably a lab treadmill run), leg strength was back within two days: plan no hard session in those two days, as the authors suggest without having tested it, then let soreness and how the legs feel decide when intensity comes back.
- **postrace-marathon-aerobic-capacity-7-days** (observational, low quality; Takayama 2017, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29138757/): In this small study, lab-measured aerobic capacity was close to pre-race a week after a marathon, so an easy first week is unlikely to cost meaningful aerobic fitness; recovery-week training was not controlled, and the study does not show that leg strength or race readiness are back.
- **postrace-marathon-damage-markers-week** (observational, low quality; Bernat-Adell 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31045685/): Expect blood markers of marathon muscle damage to take about a week to clear and inflammation markers longer; faster finishers had higher marker levels, but whether they took longer to clear was not reported. Keeping the first week free of hard sessions and races is a coach choice this study did not test.
- **postrace-marathon-strength-rest-week** (rct, low quality; Sherman 1984, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6511541/): In one tiny trial, daily runs of 20-45 minutes at about 50-60% of VO2max slowed strength recovery in the week after a marathon (the impairment was reported after the 40-45-minute runs at 60%): keep any running that week short, take rest days while the legs are weak or sore, and leave hard sessions until strength and soreness recover.
