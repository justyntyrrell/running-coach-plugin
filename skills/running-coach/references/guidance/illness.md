# Illness and return to training

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version acf61b6f40f3); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### illness-return

**Guidance:** A check-in in the last 7 days reported illness. Ask where the symptoms are and whether there is a fever. Symptoms only above the neck and no fever: easy running is reasonable, stopping if it feels worse. Fever or whole-body symptoms: no running until the fever has gone, then a gradual return. Expect about a week of symptoms; influenza, COVID and severe illness take longer.

**Purpose:** Keep a mild cold from costing more training than it needs to, and a systemic illness from being trained through.

**Uncertainty:** The neck check has never been tested as a rule. Its halves rest on one small trial of a laboratory cold, at 70% of heart-rate reserve, and on expert review; easy running is the coach's extension of that trial. Most return data come from one research group. The check-in records illness as yes or no, without symptoms or temperature, so ask.

**Alternatives:**

- One or two full rest days even with symptoms only above the neck, when the athlete feels unwell or sleeps badly.
- A doctor's review before resuming when the illness runs well past a week or worsens once running restarts.

**Claims:** ill-respiratory-symptoms-week-little-time-lost, ill-mild-infection-spares-endurance, ill-confirmed-infection-return-timeline, ill-exercise-during-head-cold-not-worse, ill-fever-no-training-until-resolved.

### illness-before-race

**Guidance:** Illness was reported in the two weeks before the race. Runners with whole-body symptoms before a distance race were about twice as likely not to finish: with such symptoms near race day, discuss not starting, and never race with a fever.

**Purpose:** Make the start decision on the illness evidence rather than on the plan.

**Uncertainty:** One race cohort, with not finishing as the outcome; its runners reported, 3-5 days before the race, symptoms of the 8-12 days before it. 14 days is a coach choice that covers that window. The context does not say which symptoms the athlete had.

**Alternatives:**

- Start at a conservative pace with a planned stopping point when symptoms are mild, above the neck and improving (untested: the cohort measured only whether symptomatic starters finished).

**Claims:** ill-systemic-symptoms-prerace-dnf, ill-fever-no-training-until-resolved.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **ill-confirmed-infection-return-timeline** (observational, low quality; Jooste 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42321006/): Across all confirmed infections the median was about 8 days to full training and 11 to full performance, and about a quarter of cases took two weeks or more to reach full performance. Influenza, COVID, severe illness and amateur status all went with longer returns than rhinovirus, mild or moderate illness or professional status, so do not expect a flu or COVID illness to fit the overall median.
- **ill-exercise-during-head-cold-not-worse** (rct, very_low quality; Weidner 1998, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9813869/): With a head cold, exercise at about 70% of heart-rate reserve (called moderate by the authors; mode not stated in the abstract) was not followed by worse symptoms than not exercising in one small trial of a laboratory rhinovirus cold (severity not graded in the abstract), which cannot rule out a modest effect; easier exercise was not tested separately. This supports the 'above the neck' half of the neck check only, not hard sessions or illness with fever or whole-body symptoms.
- **ill-fever-no-training-until-resolved** (narrative_review, very_low quality; Dick 2014, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24790692/): With a fever, do not run. Resume gradually once the fever and any dehydration have resolved; for a febrile infection with whole-body symptoms, published expert guidance ranges from resuming once the fever has gone to waiting 7-14 days after symptoms resolve. The review also reports that rest is consistently recommended with a fever of 38°C or more, and for symptomatic athletes whose resting temperature is 0.5-1°C above normal and whose pulse is 10 beats per minute above baseline.
- **ill-mild-infection-spares-endurance** (systematic_review, low quality; Kaulback 2023, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35695464/): Mild respiratory infections showed little effect on measured endurance, though athletes may feel less able to train during one; expect a larger performance cost after more severe infections and plan a graded return rather than resuming where the plan left off.
- **ill-respiratory-symptoms-week-little-time-lost** (meta_analysis, low quality; Snyders 2022, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34789459/): A typical respiratory illness should change the week, not end the block: expect about a week of symptoms and adjust training rather than abandon it. Symptoms lasting well past a week are outside the usual course; losing more than a day happens in about one episode in five.
- **ill-systemic-symptoms-prerace-dnf** (observational, low quality; Van Tonder 2016, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27343239/): Ask about symptoms in the two weeks before a race. Systemic, whole-body symptoms argue against starting; symptomatic runners overall did worse, but the risk was highest with systemic symptoms.
