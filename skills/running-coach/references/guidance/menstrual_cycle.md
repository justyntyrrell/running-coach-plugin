# Menstrual cycle phase, performance and cycle-based training in eumenorrheic women

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version acf61b6f40f3); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### cycle-phase-not-a-plan-axis

**Guidance:** Do not periodise the build by menstrual-cycle phase as a general rule: on average performance may differ only trivially between phases, and inconsistently between studies, and the one running trial of cycle-timed hard sessions found no added gain. Plan the block as usual. If the athlete has a natural cycle and reports a consistent personal pattern, or symptoms on a given day, adjust that day's session to what she reports.

**Purpose:** Keep cycle-syncing templates out of the plan unless the athlete's own experience supports them, and make symptom-led adjustment the default.

**Uncertainty:** All evidence is low quality: few studies combined the recommended phase-verification methods, the trials are small and short, and the two training trials disagree. The phase claims cover eumenorrheic or naturally menstruating women only; the symptom survey (cycle-symptoms-cost-training-days) excluded only combined hormonal contraception, so it may include progestin-only users and women with irregular cycles. The coach does not know whether she cycles naturally, uses hormonal contraception, or has irregular or absent periods; the last is a health signal the REDs consensus addresses (fuel-low-energy-availability-reds). A larger trial with hormone-verified phases (IMPACT) has not reported. The build window mirrors build-intensity-mix, a coach choice.

**Alternatives:**

- If she wants to try it, place a short block of the hardest intervals in the follicular phase, as the one positive trial did, at no cost to the rest of the plan.
- If she tracks her cycle, compare her own key sessions by phase over several cycles before changing anything.

**Claims:** cycle-early-follicular-trivial-dip, cycle-hormone-verified-effects-inconsistent, cycle-adapted-running-no-added-gain, cycle-symptoms-cost-training-days.

**Counter-evidence:** cycle-follicular-sprint-block-keeps-vo2max.

### cycle-recovery-context

**Guidance:** When HRV is among the markers off baseline, ask, if the athlete tracks a natural cycle and is happy to say, where she is in it before reading the deviation as accumulated fatigue: HRV is usually higher early in the cycle than late in it, by about 3-9%. A fall late in the cycle may be partly cyclical; one well beyond that range, or one that comes with poorer sessions or mood, still counts as fatigue.

**Purpose:** Avoid lightening training for a normal cyclical shift in a wearable marker, without dismissing real fatigue.

**Uncertainty:** The typical swing (about 3-9% in the review's abstract) is mostly below the 15% the deviation flag requires, so the cycle alone rarely triggers it, and the 28-day baseline spans about one cycle and partly absorbs it. Phases were confirmed biochemically in only 7 of the 15 studies and estimated in the rest, and hormonal contraception changes the pattern.

**Alternatives:**

- Proceed as recovery-deviations advises when she is early in her cycle, does not track it or would rather not say.

**Claims:** cycle-wearable-hrv-dips-late-cycle.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **cycle-adapted-running-no-added-gain** (rct, low quality; Kubica 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38977510/): Timing a woman's hard runs to her cycle added no endurance gain in the only running trials found: plan intensity by the block and her readiness on the day, and keep cycle-aligned scheduling as an optional preference of hers.
- **cycle-early-follicular-trivial-dip** (meta_analysis, low quality; McNulty 2020, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32661839/): Do not plan a woman's training or racing around cycle phase as a general rule: the average effect is too small and too variable to act on. Where she reports a consistent personal pattern, such as feeling flat in the first days of her period, work from her pattern rather than a phase template.
- **cycle-follicular-sprint-block-keeps-vo2max** (rct, low quality; Kissow 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40818533/): When a woman plans a short, dense block of all-out intervals, placing it in the follicular phase (from her period to ovulation) is a low-cost choice that one trial favours; it is not established enough to restructure a training plan around.
- **cycle-hormone-verified-effects-inconsistent** (systematic_review, low quality; Schlie 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40695607/): Treat any claim that one phase is best for hard training or racing as unproven, even from the better-controlled studies; a woman's own repeated experience is a better guide than a phase chart.
- **cycle-symptoms-cost-training-days** (observational, low quality; Bruinvels 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33199360/): Ask a woman whether cycle symptoms affect her training, and adjust the day's session to the symptoms she reports rather than to her cycle day.
- **cycle-wearable-hrv-dips-late-cycle** (systematic_review, low quality; de Jager 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41545627/): For a naturally cycling woman, read a modest late-cycle fall in HRV as possibly cyclical before calling it accumulated fatigue: ask where she is in her cycle, and compare like phase with like phase when judging a trend.
