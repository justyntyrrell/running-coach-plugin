# Strides, short sprints and neuromuscular training for distance runners

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version acf61b6f40f3); never edited by hand.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **strides-elite-coaches-speed-work** (elite_practice, very_low quality; Haugen 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42172297/): Successful coaches include near-maximal (>90% of max velocity) running as a supportive element, secondary to endurance work; this is practice, not proof that it works.
- **strides-explosive-training-economy** (meta_analysis, low quality; Denadai 2017, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27497600/): Explosive-type neuromuscular work is a reasonable alternative or complement to heavy lifting for economy; gains can appear within a few weeks, but longer programmes gave larger gains.
- **strides-hiit-beats-sit-for-vo2max** (meta_analysis, low quality; Xiao 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41740126/): For raising VO2max, sprint intervals do not replace long aerobic intervals (the authors suggest 3-6 repetitions of 4-5 min at 80-95% MAV, 2-3 sessions a week) as the main above-threshold stimulus; keep sprints, if used, as a small complement to that work.
- **strides-intensity-progression-injury-null** (rct, low quality; Ramskov 2018, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29527322/): Small, individually paced increments of faster running have not been shown to raise injury risk compared with adding easy volume. Introduce the first fast running gradually: the trial's early weeks leaned the other way (not significantly), when runners switched abruptly from moderate to hard pace. This does not show that maximal sprints are safe.
- **strides-short-sprints-raise-aerobic-anaerobic** (meta_analysis, low quality; Boullosa 2022, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35090181/): Very short, fast efforts gave aerobic as well as anaerobic gains over no exercise or usual training. Use them as a complement to aerobic intervals and easy volume, not a replacement: they did not differ significantly from HIIT or continuous training here, but a later meta-analysis (strides-hiit-beats-sit-for-vo2max) found HIIT better for VO2max.
- **strides-sprint-intervals-well-trained-3000m** (rct, low quality; Jin 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39981302/): Near-maximal sprint work can add a little to a trained runner's 3000 m-type performance within six weeks (about 5 s over 3000 m, roughly 1%, in this trial). This small trial could not show a change in economy or VO2max (non-significant small-to-moderate effects favoured the sprint group). Count a sprint session as one of the week's quality sessions, not an add-on to them.
