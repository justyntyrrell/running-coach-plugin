# Fueling and race nutrition

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version a596fb349f63); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### race-fueling-rehearsal

**Guidance:** Settle the race fuelling plan now and rehearse it in long runs and race-pace sessions: carbohydrate-rich eating before the race, in-race carbohydrate scaled to expected duration (little or none for about an hour, high hourly intake for a marathon), and caffeine only at a dose already tried in training.

**Purpose:** Carbohydrate availability and a trained gut protect late-race pace and limit gastrointestinal trouble.

**Uncertainty:** Benefits grow with event duration and are smaller in trained athletes; most trials are laboratory tests. Gut training timelines are unknown.

**Alternatives:**

- For a half marathon around an hour, a pre-race meal and a mouth rinse or a single gel instead of a full in-race plan.

**Claims:** fuel-race-carbohydrate-scales-with-duration, fuel-carbohydrate-during-exercise-helps, fuel-train-the-gut, fuel-caffeine-3-6-mg-kg.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **fuel-caffeine-3-6-mg-kg** (consensus, moderate quality; Guest 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33388079/): Caffeine is an option for the race if it has been tried in training at a modest dose; weigh sleep and gut effects.
- **fuel-carb-load-glycogen-rises-with-intake** (meta_analysis, low quality; Jones 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42828472/): Glycogen stores track how much carbohydrate is eaten, so loading means a clear rise in carbohydrate over a day and a half to two days before a long race, not one large pasta dinner the night before. Individual responses vary widely, and the review measured glycogen, not race times.
- **fuel-carb-load-helps-events-over-90-min** (narrative_review, low quality; Hawley 1997, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9291549/): For a race expected to last more than about 90 minutes (most half marathons for recreational runners and every marathon), eating more carbohydrate than usual in the day or two before is worth planning; for races of an hour or so, normal eating is enough. The size of the gain is an estimate from older lab studies, not a promise for any one runner.
- **fuel-carb-load-marathon-day-before-intake** (observational, low quality; Atkinson 2011, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21590642/): In a real marathon, recreational runners who ate a lot of carbohydrate the day before ran faster and slowed less late on, which supports a deliberately carbohydrate-heavy day before a marathon. It is an association from self-reported intake, so it cannot show how much of the difference the food caused.
- **fuel-carb-load-marathoners-eat-too-little** (observational, low quality; Lanpir 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41054247/): In this group, runners preparing for a marathon ate nowhere near what loading needs, so a race-week note should make loading concrete (planned meals and snacks) rather than assume it happens. It is one small group of mostly male runners, so it does not say how common under-loading is.
- **fuel-carbohydrate-120g-protects-critical-power** (rct, low quality; Norte 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42322010/): In events past about 2.5-3 hours, higher carbohydrate intake helps keep the pace a runner can hold late in the race, and more appears better than 60 g/h. Running guts tolerate less than cycling ones, so build towards higher intakes in long training runs rather than jumping to 120 g/h on race day; this is one small cycling study.
- **fuel-carbohydrate-during-exercise-helps** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Ramos-Campo 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37449467/): Taking carbohydrate in longer sessions and races is well supported; the benefit for a trained runner in a roughly 1-hour race is smaller.
- **fuel-prerace-meal-helps-prolonged-exercise** (meta_analysis, low quality; Aird 2018, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29315892/): Before a long race such as a half or full marathon, eat a familiar carbohydrate-based breakfast rather than racing fasted; before shorter races the evidence shows no clear gain, so comfort decides. Practise the breakfast before long training runs.
- **fuel-race-carbohydrate-scales-with-duration** (narrative_review, low quality; Burke 2019, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30747558/): Plan race fuelling by expected duration: for about an hour, a carbohydrate-rich pre-race meal and little or no in-race intake; for a marathon, practise high hourly intake in training.
- **fuel-train-the-gut** (narrative_review, low quality; Jeukendrup 2017, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28332114/): Rehearse the race fuelling plan, products and amounts, in long runs and race-pace sessions in the weeks before the race.
