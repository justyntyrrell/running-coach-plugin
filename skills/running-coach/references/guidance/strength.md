# Strength, plyometrics, running economy and injury prevention

Rendered from the coaching knowledge (version acf61b6f40f3); never edited by hand.

## Rules

### strength-in-build

**Guidance:** Fewer than two strength sessions were recorded last week (Monday to Sunday). Ask whether the athlete lifts without recording it; if not, build toward about two a week, favouring heavy, low-repetition lower-body lifting progressed toward near-maximal loads, with a little progressive plyometric work. Avoid routine cold-water immersion straight after lifting. Present the injury benefit in runners as uncertain.

**Purpose:** Improve running economy and late-race durability; strength effects on economy are small but consistent over 10 or more weeks.

**Uncertainty:** Economy gains are small with wide intervals, and plyometric evidence conflicts. Strength training prevents injuries across sports, but in runners exercise programmes did not clearly reduce injury except when supervised. Two is the low end of the 2-3 sessions a week the economy trials ran and the determinants review recommends, as their abstracts report. Only activities typed WeightTraining in Intervals (or strength_training by Garmin) count, so none recorded is not proof the athlete does not lift.

**Alternatives:**

- One maintenance session a week when running load is at its peak.
- Plyometrics-led work for an athlete without access to heavy lifting, accepting a smaller economy effect.

**Claims:** strength-heavy-over-plyometric-economy, strength-physiological-determinants, strength-economy-highly-trained, strength-jump-training-runners, strength-injury-prevention-all-sports, strength-runner-prevention-programs-null, recovery-cold-water-blunts-strength, strides-explosive-training-economy.

### form-metrics-descriptive

**Guidance:** The latest run recorded running-form metrics (cadence, ground contact, vertical oscillation or ratio, step length). Read them as description, never as a target for economy: single form variables explained little of the differences in economy between runners.

**Purpose:** Keep form numbers from becoming cues the evidence does not back.

**Uncertainty:** The meta-analysis is cross-sectional and between runners: it does not test changing one runner's form.

## Claims

Each with its evidence type, quality, source and link, then what it means in practice.

- **strength-biomechanics-explain-little-economy** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Van Hooren 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38446400/): Do not cue form changes (contact time, stride length) for economy; small cadence or stiffness links are not a reason to overhaul a runner's gait.
- **strength-contraception-oc-strength-adaptation-null** (meta_analysis, low quality; Nolan 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37755666/): Program strength work for a runner on the pill exactly as for a non-user: the pill is no reason to expect smaller or larger strength gains.
- **strength-economy-highly-trained** (meta_analysis, low quality; Balsalobre-Fernández 2016, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26694507/): Strength training is worth keeping in a trained runner's week; the effect is on economy, not VO2max.
- **strength-heavy-over-plyometric-economy** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Eihara 2022, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36370207/): Favour heavy, low-repetition lower-body lifting (working toward >=90% 1RM) for 2 sessions a week over 10+ weeks; plyometrics are a complement, not a substitute.
- **strength-injury-prevention-all-sports** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Lauersen 2018, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30131332/): Strength work is a plausible durability tool; this evidence comes mostly from team and field sports, so its size in runners is uncertain.
- **strength-jump-training-runners** (meta_analysis, moderate quality; Ramirez-Campillo 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33956587/): Short plyometric work (low volume, progressed) is a reasonable addition for economy and speed; introduce it gradually because it adds impact load.
- **strength-physiological-determinants** (systematic_review, moderate quality; Blagrove 2018, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29249083/): Two to three varied strength sessions a week are a reasonable default; concerns about added mass or lost aerobic fitness are not supported.
- **strength-runner-prevention-programs-null** (meta_analysis, low quality; Wu 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38261240/): Do not promise injury protection from a home strength routine; if strength is prescribed for durability, consistency and supervision or accountability may matter.
